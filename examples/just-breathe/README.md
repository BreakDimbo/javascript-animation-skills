# just breathe.

A 43-second sunset dogfight over the sea, in the manner of a hand-painted anime sky: a white fighter with elliptical wings, green-white-red roundels and a tricolour rudder, a navy rival diving out of the light, violet cumulus with gold edges, a sea full of sun, and at the end, the words *just breathe.*

This one is **3D**. `index.html` (252 KB) is a WebGL2 raymarcher: one fragment shader draws the planes (signed-distance models, cel-shaded, with an ink outline), the clouds (piles of spheres with flat bases and billow noise, rendered as a soft shell), the sea (wave normals, reflections, sun glitter), the tracers, smoke and spray. The waltz, the engines and the guns are synthesized in the same file with Web Audio. Nothing is loaded.

It keeps the pack's contract (`window.draw(frame)`, `FRAMES`, `FPS`, `CUES`, `SCORE`), so the pack's scripts render and check it unchanged:

```bash
node ../../skills/javascript-animation/scripts/render.mjs index.html just-breathe.mp4
node ../../skills/soundtrack/scripts/sync-check.mjs just-breathe.mp4 --cues-file just-breathe.cues.json
node ../../skills/javascript-animation/scripts/asset-audit.mjs index.html
```

Open `index.html` in a browser to watch it live (tap or click to start the sound). Add `?debug` to see the shot name, time and plane positions. The offline render (`?render`) draws every pixel at full quality; headless Chromium with no GPU falls back to software GL (SwiftShader), so expect several seconds a frame and a few hours for the whole film.

## Play it

`index.html?play` (or the *起飞 ›* button on the film) turns the same world into a game, with its interface in Chinese: you fly the white fighter through five levels, then an endless evening. Nobody dies: a beaten plane smokes, glides down and ditches, and when it is your turn the screen says *just breathe.* and lets you try the level again.

- **Interface: an Art Deco flight journal.** The look comes from the world (the Adriatic around 1929, travel posters, brass instruments, a sunset) rather than from a generic game kit, borrowing from games whose interface belongs to their world (the Belle Époque and Art Deco of *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33*, the minimal HUD of *Ghost of Tsushima*), from Apple's Liquid Glass (edges that catch the light) and from kinetic type and film grain on the web. The menus are HTML over the running sky: chamfered glass panels with gold corner brackets whose rim is brightest on the side facing the scene's own sun (recomputed as the camera moves), gold foil with a slow sheen for the one thing to do, a Songti display face with Didone italics and Futura capitals, Roman numerals for levels. The title *深呼吸* breathes, widening and glowing over an eight-second in-and-out, with a small *吸气 · 呼气* cue beneath, between cinematic black bars; a gold hairline lies on the real horizon and film grain moves over it. A level opens like a film's title card: black bars slide in and the name comes into focus letter by letter. In flight the HUD stays out of the way: armour and boost are two arcs around the gunsight that brighten only when they change, the score counts up in gold Didone numerals, the weapon is a medallion whose ring fills with experience, messages arrive with wide tracking and settle between two rules, rivals are gold diamonds, and the stick and buttons are a compass rose and brass knobs.
- **Controls.** Phone: drag on the left half to steer, hold the right half to fire, the small button boosts. Keyboard: arrows or WASD, space to fire, shift to boost, 1-3 to pick a card. The default *arcade* controls turn the plane directly (it banks by itself, never past 60°) and set a climb or dive of up to 40° that levels out when you let go: no rolls, no loops, the horizon never goes round. *Pilot* controls (roll and pull) are one tap away on the title screen.
- **Comfort.** The default *steady* camera keeps the horizon level (it tilts about 6° in a hard turn, where the plane banks 60°) and trails the plane's heading slightly so turns read as a bank; *dynamic* rolls with the plane. Shake is light and off when the phone asks for reduced motion. Rivals don't sit on your tail: they engage for a few seconds, break off, then come back across your nose, so the fight keeps coming into view. Aim snaps: with a rival inside 20° of the nose, the plane drifts onto the point to shoot at (your own stick always wins), the reticle slides onto it and your rounds converge there; *开火 · 自动* shoots whenever something is locked. Level 1 shows a hint until you have turned and fired.
- **Levels.** *初光*, *海峡*, *金鼻子* (a black-and-gold ace with a nose cannon and a health bar across the top), *漫长的黄昏*, *最后的光*, then endless evenings that get tougher by 15% each. Rivals appear straight ahead. The first three levels are gentle: few rivals with less armour, flying away from you at first, firing half as often and twice as wide, and their hits do half damage; the ace there has little over half its armour and a slow cannon. From level 4 they come at you head-on at full strength. Rookies fly slowly and shoot wide; veterans turn harder, lead their shots and break away when you sit on their tail. Your furthest level is remembered, so you can continue from it.
- **The weapon: 30 levels.** Kills give weapon experience (2 for a rookie, 3 for a veteran, 10 for the ace), and glowing crates dropped by beaten planes give 4; green crates repair. Each level fires about 2.5% faster and hits 5% harder, and new things arrive along the way, each brighter than the last: incendiary rounds (5), four guns (8), fast tracers (10), explosive rounds (12), homing rockets (15), six guns (18), violet plasma that goes through up to three planes (20), rocket salvos (22), chain lightning that jumps to two more rivals (24), wings of light (25), a beam cannon that burns whatever it touches while you fire (26), a missile storm that fans out a dozen missiles (28), and at 30 *凤凰* (phoenix): rainbow rounds, a faster rainbow storm and a trail of golden embers. Tracers get wider and hotter with the level, muzzle flashes take the weapon's colour, and from level 12 kills throw a coloured shockwave. A level-up shows *武器升级 Lv.N* with a ring and an arpeggio; an unlock adds its name and a chord. Trails fade as they pass the camera so the effects never cover the screen. The level is kept from one stage to the next within a run.
- **Between levels** you get a bonus for the level and for the armour you kept, and choose one of three cards: faster guns, hot barrels, long rounds, thicker skin, afterburner, magnet sights, or an armoury (weapon +3 levels).
- **Feel.** Hits flash the rival white, throw sparks in your weapon's colour (more of them at higher levels), ping, and mark the reticle. A kill holds the world still for a tenth of a second, then a fireball, tumbling debris, a shockwave ring, a boom with a sub-bass drop, and the score rising from the wreck; kills within five seconds of each other build a combo up to ×4. Gunshots are layered (a bright crack, a thump that drops in pitch, a mechanical tick), enemy rounds that pass close whizz, the afterburner roars, low armour sounds an alarm, and the ace arrives with timpani.

## Play together

*联机 · 合作 / 对战* on the title screen opens a lobby for up to four players, together against the rivals (*合作*, co-op) or against each other (*对战*, versus). One player opens a room (it gets a four-digit number) and picks the mode; the others see it listed with its mode and tap to join. Everyone flies their own fighter in their own livery and callsign: 白鹭 (white, the host), 赤狐 (crimson), 青燕 (sky blue) and 金雀 (gold). Names float over wingmen, off-screen wingmen get an arrow, and the top-left corner lists the crew with their armour.

- **Who runs what.** The host's page flies the rivals, runs the waves, drops the crates and decides when a level starts, ends or fails. Every page flies its own plane and its own weapon (all 30 levels, beam and missiles included) and draws everyone else's from what their pages report. A guest's hits on a rival are added up and sent to the host, which applies them; rivals fire on every page from where they are drawn, and each page decides whether its own plane was hit. Kills count for everyone: each player gains the weapon experience, and the score is shared.
- **In a crew.** Rivals get 60% more armour for each extra player and each picks one of you to chase. A player who ditches rides behind a wingman for six seconds, then comes back in behind them; the level fails only when everyone is in the water. Between levels each player picks their own card; the host waits for the others (or 25 seconds).
- **How it travels.** Each player is one *presence*: a small object of absolute state (position and orientation, armour, weapon level, counters of rounds, rockets and missile storms fired, damage dealt to each rival, crates taken) sent about 30 times a second. The host's also carries the game state, the rivals and the crates (about 1.2 KB with eight rivals and eight crates). Other planes are drawn 110 ms in the past, interpolated between the last two states received, so they move smoothly at any frame rate.
- **Versus.** No rivals: everyone starts even with the weapon at Lv.8 and full armour, on a ring around the middle of the sky, facing in. Aim snap and every weapon (missiles, chain lightning, the beam) work on other players, who are marked like rivals with their callsign and distance. A kill gives 6 weapon experience and a death 2, and crates drop now and then, so the weapon climbs through the match. A player shot down (or into the sea) comes back three seconds later somewhere on the ring, with two seconds' grace. The first to five kills wins; a kill feed announces each one, the tally sits in the top-left corner, and the host can call a rematch or go back to the room to switch modes.
- **Who decides a hit in versus.** The shooter's page does: it flies its own rounds against the other planes as it draws them and adds up the damage it dealt to each player. The target's page reads that tally from the shooter's presence and applies it, at a quarter strength so a fight lasts, to its own armour, and reports its deaths with who hit it last. Every page writes the kill feed from those reports; the host keeps the score.
- **Where it works.** In the page published on claude.ai, the room is the page's `room` capability: anyone signed in who can open the page (so share it with your friends first) can join. Opened as a plain file, the lobby only reaches other tabs of the same browser, which is enough to try it out.

## Running on a phone

The live film and the game use a lighter path than the offline render, built for phone GPUs:

- **Baked sky.** At load, the sky (sun, cirrus, islands, rays) and the sky-with-clouds are painted once into two panoramas, a strip per frame. The sea reflects the panorama with one lookup instead of marching the clouds again, and the horizon haze is read from it too.
- **Clouds at low resolution.** Clouds are soft, so they are marched into a buffer at 40% of the render resolution, with the depth of their front, and composited at full resolution: planes still fly in front of, behind and into them.
- **Particles as geometry.** Smoke, spray, tracers, vapour and gulls are instanced quads, depth-tested against the raymarched scene, instead of loops inside every pixel.
- **Dynamic resolution.** The render scale moves between 30% and 100% of the screen to hold the frame rate; a post pass grades and upscales.
- **Cheaper CPU work.** Per-cloud values live in uniforms; flight frames are memoized.

It has been checked in headless Chromium (software GL) for correctness, not yet on an iPhone.


## How it is built

- **One world, many cameras.** Both flight paths are integrated once at load from "turtle" programs (speed, turn rate, pitch rate) plus a keyed altitude curve. Orientation comes from the path itself: forward is the velocity and up is the lift (acceleration plus gravity), averaged over 1.2 s and weighted by its strength, so turns bank, banks roll in and out at a believable rate, and the loop goes inverted without any hand-set angles. Chase cameras follow a frame averaged over a quarter second, so they glide. The rival rides the hero's own path 1.3 s behind, overshoots when the hero loops, and after the hit glides down to a belly landing on the water. The cloud the hero dives through is placed on the hero's path.
- **Ten shots on a 3/4 bar grid** (96 bpm, 1.875 s a bar): a calm opening where the fighter roars over the camera into the sun, a profile of the livery, the rival dropping out of the clouds, tracers, the dive through a cumulus, the loop seen against the sun, the reversal and the hit, the ditching seen from the water, one silent bar skimming past the floating rival, and the ending, where the camera holds on the sun, the words come up, and the hero passes overhead into the light.
- **Details.** The cumulus are piles of spheres with big round lobes grown onto them, then a shallower layer of small ones (lattices of random balls, blended into the surface), with feathered edges. They are lit the way a background painter would: three tones with soft steps (blue-violet shadow, rose middle, cream-gold light) chosen from a normal that follows the big lobes, a warm band where light turns to shadow, and a faint horizontal brush grain. The fighters have a lofted fuselage (a long cowling running into a pointed spinner), and carry exhaust stubs with soot streaks, wing cannons, an antenna mast, a tail wheel, and panel and control-surface lines that fade out when they get smaller than a pixel.
- **Small things.** A pilot in a leather cap sits behind a glass canopy that reflects the sky; the airframe casts its own shadows (wing on fuselage, tail on tailplane); the paint picks up the sky; exhausts flicker close up; spent cartridge cases tumble out of the hero's wings as it fires; the rival's engine coughs after the hit and dies on the water. Rocky islands sit in the haze on the horizon and in the water, low streaks of cloud lie along it, soft rays fan out of the sun, and a few gulls circle in the opening and the ending, with their calls in the soundtrack.
- **The fight.** Wingtip vapour trails appear only under high g (the break, the loop, the pull-outs). The rival opens fire as it dives and keeps firing through the chase; tracers are streaks, bullets that pass close to the camera whizz, cameras riding with a firing plane shake a little, and the hit throws a flash and tumbling shards. Motes of haze streak past the chase cameras to sell the speed. The rival rolls into its dive (its dive eases into the chase path with no kink in speed or acceleration, so it arrives without a shudder), and the hero rolls once as it flies into the sun.
- **Water that reacts.** Each plane leaves a trail of short wake segments over its last 3 s, weighted by how low and fast it flies. The sea shader turns them into ripples spreading out to both sides, ruffled dark water with sun glints under the prop wash, and foam only where something touches the water. Spray rises behind a low pass, and the rival's belly landing throws up a plume and leaves a sliding wake.
- **Sound follows the world.** Each engine's gain, pan and Doppler shift are computed from the camera and the plane at 30 points a second, and the gunfire is placed the same way. The music is a waltz in D minor (musette accordion, piano, strings, brushes). It stops for the silent bar and resolves to D major for the ending.

The planes and the scene are original designs made for this piece. The mood is borrowed, not the characters.
